Share the photo, not your location.

Photos quietly carry the GPS coordinates of where they were taken, the exact time, your phone’s model and sometimes its serial number. Stripper removes all of it and gives the file a brand-new fingerprint, so the copy you post can’t be matched to your original.

What gets removed

Location

GPS coordinates and altitude, plus any city, region or country written into the file.

Date & time

When the photo was taken, edited and saved, including your time zone.

Your device

Phone or camera model, lens, serial numbers, owner name and maker-specific data.

Hidden extras

Embedded thumbnails (which can show the photo before you cropped it), depth maps and motion-photo video.

Edits & notes

Editing software, edit history, comments, captions, keywords, author and copyright.

File fingerprint

Every byte is rebuilt and a few pixels shift invisibly, so the checksum (SHA-256, MD5…) is new every time.

How it works

  1. Inspect. Stripper reads the file and shows you exactly what it gives away.
  2. Rebuild. It keeps only the visible pixels, the right way up, and nudges a tiny random patch by 1/255. You can’t see it, but every copy is unique.
  3. Verify. The new file is scanned again to confirm nothing survived, and you can compare the before and after fingerprints.

Good to know

  • The picture itself can still give you away. A street sign, the view from your window, a reflection or a recognisable landmark says more than any tag. Crop before you post.
  • Reverse image search still works. A new checksum stops exact-file matching, but services like Google Lens compare what a picture looks like. If you posted the same photo under your name somewhere else, it can still be found.
  • Most big sites strip GPS on upload, but not all do, and you can’t see what they keep for themselves. Cleaning first means they never receive it.
  • Want to check it’s really offline? Load this page, switch on airplane mode and use it. It keeps working, because nothing is sent anywhere.
